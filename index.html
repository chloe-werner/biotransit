How the design works

Three compartments. One clear handoff.

01

Separated compartments

Three separated compartments support different temperature and humidity conditions, so items with different needs travel together without sharing one environment.

02

Prepared before packing

Each compartment reaches its selected environment automatically during preparation. Plan on about one hour per compartment.

03

Labels that tell staff what to do

Prominent labels show the required temperature, where the contents should be stored, and what to check on arrival. Receiving staff do not have to track down the sender to know what comes next.

The reuse cycle

Every trip follows the same four steps, so cost is measured per successful shipment, not per box.

  1. 1 Prepare and pack Set each compartment, secure the contents and fill in the labels.
  2. 2 Ship Passive insulation protects the shipment through loading, transit and unloading.
  3. 3 Receive and check Staff follow the label: check condition, then move contents to the named destination.
  4. 4 Return and inspect The box comes back, is cleaned and inspected, and is ready for its next trip.

Application 01

Cell research labs

A research team sends a defined shipment of samples or reagents to another lab. When the box arrives, the people receiving it know exactly where the contents belong, even when the usual lab contact is away.

  1. The sender Secures and labels Vials or plates are secured in their compartment, and the label states the required temperature and storage destination.
  2. On arrival No guessing at receiving Instead of a box marked only "perishable," staff see what to check and where each item goes.
  3. Why it matters Research keeps moving Clear instructions reduce uncertainty at receiving and help avoid delays caused by samples waiting for the right person.

Application 02

Pharmaceutical transport

A supplier or distributor delivers a temperature-sensitive medicine to a hospital pharmacy. Repeatable packing and clear receiving checks support the pharmacy's own acceptance process.

  1. The supplier Packs the same way every time A repeatable packing process means each delivery on the route leaves in the same condition.
  2. At the pharmacy Checks against acceptance criteria Pharmacy staff review shipment condition and any agreed temperature records against their own acceptance criteria.
  3. Why it matters A failed delivery affects patients When a delivery is rejected, replacement product takes time, and scheduled care can be pushed back.

Our story

It began with fresh produce.

BioTransit began as a produce shipping concept for farms and businesses that deliver directly to customers. The engineers also saw potential in meal delivery, and early research looked at services like HelloFresh before the focus moved to labs and pharmaceutical transport.

The same questions carried over to labs and pharmacies: how do you protect what is inside, and how does the person who opens the box know what to do next?

Produce design goals and options

  • Up to seven days of freshness for produce, as a design goal
  • Humidity management to suit different produce types
  • Removable dividers for shipping a single produce type
  • Disposable ethylene adsorbent packets to help manage a gas that can speed up ripening

These were goals and options explored for produce. They are not performance claims for lab or pharmaceutical shipments.

Buyer FAQ

Questions buyers ask first.

Can I buy it outright?

Yes. BioTransit is sold as a purchase, starting at $1,799 per container. Ask about rental if you only need units for a limited trial.

How heavy is it?

BioTransit weighs 42.14 lb empty. Your staff can try lifting, packing and carrying it during a trial on your route.

What temperatures can it hold?

That depends on the configuration, payload and route. Before you buy, we test the configuration against your required range and share the results with you.

Who handles returns?

You do. Once you buy BioTransit, the containers are yours, and your team decides how they get back to you after each trip. Every container has a serial number, and we train your staff on cleaning and inspection so each one is ready for its next shipment.

How is it cleaned between trips?

Your team cleans and inspects each container after every trip using our procedure, and records the inspection against its serial number. Units that fail inspection are repaired or retired.

How many containers will I need?

It depends on how often you ship and how long a full round trip takes. For example, 4 shipments a week on a two week cycle needs about 8 containers, plus a few spares. We size this with you during your quote.

How does a trial work?

We agree on your cargo, temperature limits, trip length and what counts as a successful delivery. Then BioTransit runs on your route alongside your current packaging, so you can compare the results before you buy.

Is it certified for my medicine or samples?

Tell us your quality requirements and we will share test data for your route. Your quality team sets the acceptance criteria, and the trial is measured against them.
